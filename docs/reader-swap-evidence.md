# What changes when you choose a different reader?

You can give the classifier your folder definitions and use it before you have a labelled training collection. Jev checks the category and its confidence. A separate reader checks the document against your definitions and supplies evidence. The system automatically files qualifying agreements. You get a review queue for uncertain documents, a reason for each processing failure and a record of each filing.

**A reader that is weaker on its own can still deliver high precision on the documents the system files.** On 941 matched news articles, 99.20% of mini’s automatic filings and 98.65% of Claude’s matched the benchmark. Mini correctly filed 54 fewer articles, sent 30 more to review and had 29 more processing failures. A user choosing a reader cares about all three changes. [Matched BBC reader comparison](bbc-mini-comparison.md)

“Zero-shot” here means the benchmarks required no project-specific model training or tuning on their evaluation labels. The system still receives category descriptions, exclusions and examples. Public documents may have appeared in vendor pretraining. Performance on a new collection depends on its categories, documents and the two judges’ errors.

## Was the smaller reader actually weaker?

On the 941 matched BBC articles, Claude alone returned exactly the reference category 880 times and mini 810 times, counting a missing answer as a miss. Claude alone was right 83 times when mini was not, and mini alone was right 13 times. The two-sided exact paired p-value was 1.20 × 10⁻¹³. Restricting the comparison to the 910 articles with two usable reader answers gave the same direction, 57 versus 12 discordant articles (p = 3.74 × 10⁻⁸).

| Outcome on the same 941 articles | Claude | Mini | Difference with mini |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Correct automatic filings | 801 | 747 | −54 |
| Wrong automatic filings | 11 | 6 | −5 |
| Review | 128 | 158 | +30 |
| Processing failure | 1 | 30 | +29 |

Of the 801 articles correctly filed under Claude, 38 went to review and 22 failed processing with mini. Six other articles moved into a correct filing with mini. All six mini misfiles were also misfiled by Claude. The decline in correct filing yield was 5.74 percentage points (conservative 95% paired interval −8.19 to −3.21). Increases in review and failure each survived Holm correction across the six BBC endpoints. Changing the unit from article to batch also gave evidence of lower correct filing yield (p = 0.000061).

The estimate of wrong filings **among those filed** moved from 1.35% to 0.80%. The conservative 95% interval for mini minus Claude spans −2.33 to +1.27 percentage points. On **all 941 articles**, the paired wrong-filing test used five discordant misfiles and gave p = 0.0625. The data support high observed precision for both readers. A claim of equal safety would need a tolerable increase in error specified in advance and a study able to rule it out. [Complete endpoint tables](reader-swap-statistical-tables.md)

## Does the second judgment catch confident mistakes?

The hypergeometric test asks how many wrong Jev choices would be withheld if the same number of eligible documents were withheld uniformly at random. A detection shows selective withholding on that benchmark. It does not establish how a future taxonomy will behave.

- **BBC with mini.** Among 803 BBC articles with usable judgments and a named Jev choice passing both confidence checks, 15 choices were wrong. The pipeline withheld 50 eligible articles and caught nine of those mistakes. Random withholding would catch 0.93 on average. One-sided p = 2.58 × 10⁻⁸. The comparable Claude analysis caught five of 16 mistakes among eight withheld articles (0.16 expected, p = 7.75 × 10⁻⁸).
- **October research papers.** Among 1,078 high-certainty named choices, the remaining rules withheld 72 and caught 17 of 40 wrong choices (2.67 expected, p = 4.23 × 10⁻¹¹). After Jev’s own yes/no check, the final stage caught seven of the remaining 30 mistakes while withholding 22 of 1,028 papers (0.64 expected, p = 1.09 × 10⁻⁶). A reading note can also prevent an automatic filing.
- **November research papers.** Among eligible choices with a usable reader answer, Sol withheld endorsement on 72 papers and caught eight wrong choices (1.82 expected), Terra three among 52 (1.23 expected), and Flash four among 57 (1.33 expected). Raw one-sided p-values were 0.000297, 0.122 and 0.041. Holding the number withheld fixed **within each subject** raised the expected counts to 4.02, 2.38 and 2.78. The subject-adjusted p-values were 0.031, 0.433 and 0.295. None cleared Holm adjustment for the three readers. Some apparent selectivity is associated with which subjects get reviewed.

The original BBC headline of eight errors caught among 14 withheld compared the final rule to a broader high-certainty baseline. It includes the no-category choice. It is evidence about the combination of rules. The named-choice rows above are the cleaner comparison for the contribution after Jev's own checks. [Original baseline](accuracy-test-bbc-news.md)

## What generalizes across readers?

Sol and low-reasoning Flash both returned outcomes for all 2,032 eligible research papers. Flash correctly filed 17 more, misfiled one more, sent 20 fewer to review and had two more processing failures. Their filing precision was 97.89% and 97.84% respectively. The point estimate of the Flash-minus-Sol misfile rate among filings was +0.04 percentage points (conservative 95% interval −1.72 to +1.80). On jointly usable reader answers Sol was ahead by 31 of 2,024. The nominal paired p-value of 0.0472 becomes 0.708 in the 18-endpoint family for the three large-arm comparisons. The previous five-test sensitivity answered a narrower question and gave 0.236. [Full Flash comparison](flash-reader-comparison-2026-11-17.md)

On the common 2,020 papers for Sol, Terra and Flash, the multi-reader test gave p ≈ 0.17 for correct filings and p ≈ 0.94 for wrong filings. Processing failure showed more evidence of differences (p ≈ 0.0027, omnibus-family Holm ≈ 0.032). Twelve Terra outcomes were missing. Luna’s 117 completed outcomes and the matched 60-paper pilots have separate analyses. Their incomplete or selected coverage limits broader conclusions. Jev was called afresh in each configuration, so paired filing comparisons measure the deployed system as a whole. [Earlier reader study](reader-study-2026-11-13.md)

**The same subjects repeatedly caused mistakes.** For Flash, 24 of 285 filings for papers whose reference subject was information theory or systems/control were wrong (8.42%), versus eight of 1,199 across the other six subjects (0.67%). The two-sided Fisher p-value was 6.57 × 10⁻¹². Sol had 23/276 versus 8/1,190, and Terra 26/285 versus 7/1,183. The October papers showed the same concentration. These studies reuse the underlying corpus. The historical choice of this pair of subjects was informed by its observed errors. [October category breakdown](accuracy-test-arxiv-papers.md)

**Cost differences were more consistent than timing differences.** Across 32 identical, fully accounted 60-paper batches, Flash cost less than Sol in every batch (median saving $2.58, exact sign p = 4.66 × 10⁻¹⁰). Flash took less elapsed time in 16 and more in 16. Terra cost less than Sol in all 29 comparable batches and finished sooner in 28. Flash cost less than Terra in all 30 comparable batches and took longer in 29. These are recorded charges and end-to-end times from different campaign periods, builds and admission conditions. Campaign totals include work outside these matched subsets.

The earlier confidence-model trial answers a different purchasing question. Jev answered 393 papers Liquid could not, and produced 708 more correct high-confidence candidates alongside 29 more wrong ones. Correctness on papers both answered differed by 21 labels out of 1,645. That contrast did not clear the original five-test Bonferroni correction (p = 0.230). Neither candidate set had passed a reader in that experiment. [Confidence-model report](decision-bakeoff-full-results-2026-11-05.md)

## Methods, uncertainty and reproduction

The supplement uses only retained evidence. The BBC pairing checks original and upload hashes for 941 articles. November pipeline pairs require matching document fingerprints and upload hashes. The confidence-model pilot keeps news, main papers and selected size failures separate. The full confidence-model study uses 2,038 eligible papers and the reader study uses 2,032 under its own exclusion rule. The research reference is the author-selected primary subject. This is an exploratory reanalysis of completed studies. [Corpus selection and limitations](arxiv-bakeoff-methodology-review.md)

- **Paired endpoints.** Exact two-sided McNemar tests use discordant pairs for correct filings, misfiles, any filing, review, processing failure and exact single-category reader answers. A and B denote the two arms in the stated order. Reader-only denominators require two usable answers unless labelled otherwise. Each 4×4 transition table uses the order correct filing, wrong filing, review, failure. The confidence-model rows similarly distinguish valid answers, correct choices and admitted candidates. [McNemar test definition](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/generated/statsmodels.stats.contingency_tables.mcnemar.html)
- **Effect sizes.** For paired event-rate differences we subtract simultaneous exact binomial intervals for each of the two discordant proportions. For misfile rates among each arm’s own filings we do the same with the two marginal proportions. Each component interval has 97.5% coverage, yielding at least 95% joint coverage by Bonferroni under independently sampled documents, without requiring the two arms to be independent. These pointwise intervals are deliberately conservative. All rates on the frozen corpus are directly observed. Extrapolation to new collections needs further evidence.
- **Selective withholding and category differences.** One-sided hypergeometric tails test enrichment at fixed margins. November “reader” tests count absence of singleton endorsement among eligible choices with a usable reader. “Pipeline” tests count every eligible Jev choice withheld from filing, including failed or missing reader answers on recorded outcomes. October “sureNamed” requires a high-certainty named choice and usable judgments, and “afterNoul” additionally requires Jev's own positive yes/no threshold. The subject-adjusted test convolves the eight fixed-margin null distributions. Two-sided Fisher tests compare category and historical subgroup tables. For these rows the two fractions in the appendix give errors/events out of the first and second groups. [Fisher exact test definition](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/stats/html/fisher.test.html)
- **Repeated readers and batches.** The multi-reader test uses Cochran’s Q with a within-document label permutation reference (19,999 replicates, fixed seed, plus-one p-value). It uses only the documents shared by every arm in that comparison. Constant outcome patterns contain no information. Original-batch sensitivity tests flip the sign of the entire paired batch difference and enumerate its exact null distribution. These checks rely on exchangeability under the null. Historical campaigns were observational. Batch-level sign tests for elapsed time and cost retain unique starts and successful completions on the same 60 documents with settled accounting. The three pairings have different eligible batch sets.
- **Multiplicity.** The appendix gives raw p-values, Holm-adjusted values within each named family, and a second Holm adjustment across all 205 computable contrasts. Twenty displayed comparisons have no informative variation for the specified enrichment or omnibus test. Empty common-valid confidence cohorts are excluded. Binary comparisons with zero discordants have p = 1 by convention. The hypotheses overlap and several cohorts are nested. The count of tests is not a count of independent replications. The broad adjustment is a sensitivity analysis. The original studies retain their own corrections. [Holm adjustment](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/stats/html/p.adjust.html)
